Solutions for hospitality spaces

Types of hospitality curtains we make

Every hospitality space asks something different of its windows. Here is how we usually approach the main areas.

01 / Hospitality

Hotel guest room curtains

Guests expect quiet, dark, and private rooms. We pair a heavy blackout or lined drape with a sheer layer, so a guest can choose between soft daylight and full darkness.

02 / Hospitality

Lobby and reception curtains

Lobbies carry your identity. Full-height drapes in rich textures, such as velvet, jacquard, or heavy woven fabrics, add scale and warmth, while tall windows get proportions that feel deliberate rather than overwhelming.

03 / Hospitality

Restaurant and café curtains

Dining rooms need glare control without making the space feel closed in. Light-filtering fabrics soften strong sun at lunch, while easy-care materials cope with cooking odors and frequent cleaning.

04 / Hospitality

Resort and villa curtains

Resort suites often open onto balconies, gardens, or water views. We recommend layered treatments with a lightweight daytime layer for the view and a thicker night layer for privacy and light control.

05 / Hospitality

Banquet hall and event space curtains

Large halls call for generous fullness, consistent color across wide runs, and fabrics that photograph well. Stage-side and divider curtains can be planned to match the main drapery.

06 / Hospitality

Conference and meeting room curtains

Presentations need controlled light. Blackout or dim-out curtains help screens stay readable, and a neutral palette keeps the room professional.

07 / Hospitality

Spa, wellness, and lounge curtains

Calm spaces benefit from soft, flowing fabrics in restful tones, with enough opacity to protect privacy during treatments.

08 / Hospitality

Serviced apartment curtains

Longer stays mean residents notice comfort. We balance homely softness with the durability a managed property needs.

Designed for hospitality performance

Why curtain selection matters in hospitality interiors

In a home, a poor curtain choice is an annoyance. In a hospitality business, it can affect reviews, repeat bookings, and replacement costs. Careful selection helps you:

  • Protect guest sleep: Effective light blocking is one of the simplest ways to improve a guest's night and their review.
  • Keep your design consistent: Curtains that follow your palette, materials, and brand style hold a property together from lobby to corridor to room.
  • Control heat and glare: Lined and thicker fabrics reduce solar heat, which helps cooling comfort in Bangladesh's warm climate.
  • Maintain privacy: Ground-floor rooms, road-facing windows, and treatment rooms need dependable coverage.
  • Reduce running costs: Fabrics chosen for abrasion resistance and easy cleaning last longer, so you replace less often.
  • Support safety requirements: Flame-retardant fabrics and linings can be specified where your property or local requirements call for them.

Built for commercial use

What makes a curtain suitable for hospitality use

Commercial-grade performance comes from a handful of decisions made before stitching begins.

  • Fabric durability: Tightly woven polyester blends, performance velvets, and heavy weaves tolerate daily use better than delicate fabrics.
  • Light control: Blackout lining, dim-out options, or sheer layers, chosen room by room.
  • Fire safety: Inherently flame-retardant or treated fabrics are available to specify. Please tell us the standard your property needs so we can match the right material.
  • Cleaning and care: Washable or dry-cleanable fabrics suit housekeeping schedules. We note care instructions for your team.
  • Color stability: Sun-facing windows demand fabrics that resist fading.
  • Hardware: Tracks, rods, and brackets sized to the curtain weight and the length of the run, so operation stays smooth.
  • Consistency across quantities: Ordering many rooms at once means matching fabric lots, pleat style, and finishing across every set.
