Blackout vs. sheer curtains: Which should you choose?

Blackout vs. sheer curtains is a choice every home in Bangladesh has to make, because our windows face harsh sun, glare, close neighbors, and dull monsoon skies, all in one year. Choose wrongly and the room pays for it: a bedroom that wakes you at sunrise, a living room that feels dark and closed in, or a window that shows everything once the lights come on.

The fix is to match the curtain to the job. Blackout curtains give you darkness, privacy, and heat control. Sheer curtains give you soft daylight and an airy look, and layering both gives you everything. This guide shows how to choose for each room. What are blackout curtains?

Blackout curtains are made from tightly woven or multi-layered fabric, often with a dense lining, so very little daylight passes through. Good ones are heavy, hang in deep folds and feel substantial in the hand. The weight is what stops the light, and it is also what gives them a rich, tailored look.

Where blackout curtains shine

  • Better sleep.  A dark room helps you sleep later into a bright morning and nap through a sunny afternoon, which matters for shift workers and light sleepers.
  • Strong privacy.  Nobody can see in, by day or by night, whatever the lighting is inside.
  • Glare control.  They protect screens, sofas, wooden floors, and artwork from direct sun.
  • Some insulation.  A thick, lined curtain adds a barrier against heat coming through the glass, and it softens street noise slightly.

What to watch for

  • A room can feel closed in with the curtains drawn all day.
  • Light leaks around the edges unless the curtain is wide enough and fitted close to the wall or ceiling.
  • Heavy fabric needs a strong rod or track and takes more care to clean.

What are sheer curtains?

Sheer curtains are lightweight, semi-transparent panels in fabrics such as voile, chiffon, organza, or linen-look weaves. They let daylight pass through while softening it, so the room feels bright without the sharp glare of direct sun.

Where sheer curtains shine

  • Natural light, gently filtered.  Rooms feel open, warm, and larger than they are.
  • Daytime privacy.  From the street, the sheer blur obscures what is inside, while you can still see out.
  • Airy elegance.  Flowing fabric softens hard window frames and works with modern, classic, and minimalist interiors.
  • Easy care.  Lightweight fabrics are usually simple to take down, wash, and re-hang.

What to watch for

  • They do not block light for sleep, and they offer little protection from heat or fading.
  • At night, with lamps on inside and darkness outside, people outside can often see in.
  • Thin fabric looks flat unless it is cut generously full, so budget for extra width.

How Bangladesh's climate changes the choice

A curtain that works in a temperate country does not always work in Dhaka, Chattogram or Sylhet. Three local factors matter most.

  • Strong sun and heat.  On east- and west-facing windows, a lined blackout curtain helps keep afternoon heat and glare out, which can ease the load on your air conditioner. See our guide to   summer curtains .
  • Monsoon grey and humidity.  In the rainy months the sky is dull and the air is damp. Sheers let in whatever light there is and dry quickly, while heavy fabrics need good airflow to avoid a musty smell. Our   rainy season curtains  page covers suitable fabrics.
  • Dust and city noise.  Urban windows collect dust and street sound. A lined blackout layer reduces both a little, while a washable sheer is easy to refresh each season.

The best of both: layering blackout and sheer curtains

If you cannot decide, you do not have to. Layering puts a sheer curtain closest to the glass and a blackout curtain in front of it, each on its own track or rod. You get daylight and daytime privacy with the sheer drawn, full darkness with the blackout drawn, and a rich, dressed window either way.

Tips for a layered setup

  • Use a double track or double rod  so each layer slides independently.
  • Hang the sheer on the inner track and the blackout on the outer one.
  • Make both layers wide enough to look full when closed; a good rule is roughly two to two and a half times the window width for sheers.
  • Choose a blackout color that complements your walls and a sheer in a soft white, ivory, or a tone close to your palette.

How to decide: five quick questions

Answer these for each window, and the right curtain usually becomes clear. If your answers differ from window to window, that is normal. Treat each window on its own, and keep fabric colors consistent across the home so it feels cohesive.

  1. Do you need darkness to sleep or watch films?  If yes, choose blackout.
  2. Does the window face east or west?  Direct morning or afternoon sun points to a lined blackout layer.
  3. How close are your neighbors to the street?  Close and overlooked windows need night privacy, which a sheer alone cannot give.
  4. How much daylight do you want in the room?  If the answer is "as much as possible," lead with a sheer.
  5. Who uses the room, and when?  A bedroom used mostly at night and a living room used all day call for different curtains.
